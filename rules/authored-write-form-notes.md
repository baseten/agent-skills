# Notes — authored-write-form

Reasoning for `rules/authored-write-form.md`. Deliberately *not* bundled into
each skill: ten copies of 2,300 words of design history is cost with no reader,
and the audience for it is a person editing the rule, who has this checkout.

Why the rules in `SKILL.md` are shaped the way they are. Moved here with the
rule itself, from `backlog-orchestrator/NOTES.md`, where it was reasoning about
a section that no longer lives there.

Some of it argues against `backlog-orchestrator`, *Posting identity*, which is
still in that skill because it maintains state across dispatched workers rather
than being a rule on its own. The two are read together where they interact.

**Why the footer exists at all, given Posting identity:** that section makes the run *report* who its writes appeared to come from; it does nothing to make a reader of the forge able to tell. On its degraded path — the common one — every comment carries the invoking user's login and `author_association: OWNER`, and nothing in the comment marks it as agent-written. A reviewer reading a reply in the owner's voice has no way to know they are not talking to the owner, which is the same attribution-honesty problem one layer out, and the one a footer actually closes.

**Why the footer marks unread writes rather than every write (the owner's ruling, Sept 2026):** the first version of this section stamped everything the run typed. A marker on every write carries no information. The reader who matters is deciding how much scrutiny a write has already had, and a blanket banner cannot separate the two cases that differ most — an unedited automated reply, where they should read closely because nobody has, from a body its author drafted with an agent, edited, and posted under their own name. So the test is whether this run obtained the person's approval of *this exact text* before posting it.

Three things made the blanket version actively wrong rather than merely uninformative. Attended work is **already** attributed durably: commits carry `Co-Authored-By: Claude`, so a banner on the description adds nothing a reader could not get from the history. A `Generated by Claude Code` stamp on a description its author owns **misstates authorship in the other direction** — and it invites a reviewer to skip the description, which is precisely the failure the source of the brevity rules is about (*The crisis of authorship at Close*; see the entry on where the PR-body numbers come from). And the avatar argument above, which is the reason the footer exists, is an argument about the **unattended** case only: it says a reader cannot tell that nobody reviewed this text. It does not reach a write a person authored under their own name.

The direction of the default matters and is the safe one: approval means the person read *this text*, so authorising the run, approving a plan, asking for the PR, or merely being in the session are all No. Where the run cannot say the text was read, the footer goes on.

Two consequences worth naming because they are changes, not restatements. `settle-outstanding-decisions`'s recorded ruling **is the one write whose footer is decided by a condition rather than by its kind**: the owner supplies the answer inside it, and the skill composes the question as asked, the dated marker and what the ruling confirms — so the test is asked of the complete comment, and the footer is omitted only where the owner was shown and approved that whole record. A first version of this entry said the ruling simply carries no footer, on the grounds that the marker already attributes it; both halves were wrong, and the reasoning is in that skill's own NOTES. And the `NEEDS_USER` draft's footerlessness stops being a special case sitting beside the rule and becomes a consequence of it. The review trigger keeps its exemption and now has two independent reasons; the functional one is the one to state, because it is the one that fails silently.

Both negation guards survive untouched — the footer is still not identity evidence, and still not a discriminator for whether a comment is the run's own. Narrowing which writes are marked changes nothing about what the mark may be used for, and the discriminator guard actually gets stronger: it would now break on every attended write.

**What the forge avatar tracks, and why it is not the signal:** on GitHub, a write made through an installed GitHub App acting on behalf of a user renders as the user's avatar with the app's badge on it — the "two icons" a reader sees intermittently. A write made through the user's own credential (a local `gh` on a PAT, say) renders as the plain avatar with no badge. Both are the same run doing the same thing; what differs is only which transport authored that particular write, and transport precedence is chosen for reliability rather than for how it renders. So the badge appears and disappears across a single run's writes, and its absence means nothing at all. That is why attribution is carried by text the run writes rather than by anything the forge draws — and it is also why the footer is explicitly *not* identity evidence: it is the run's own claim, and reading it back as an observation would let the run establish its identity by asserting it, which is exactly what Posting identity's observation rule refuses.

**Why the screenshot rule is conditional on the repository providing a capture (Sept 2026):** it arrived unconditional, ported from #71, which took it from a human author's PR-description style guide. That is the right rule *there*: the actor is a person at a machine who can always take a screenshot. This contract's actor is often a dispatched worker in a container with no browser and no harness, and an obligation an actor cannot meet is not met — it is discharged with a fabricated "N/A", or worse with a claim of a visual check nobody performed, which is the same attribution dishonesty the footer exists to prevent pointed at behaviour instead of authorship.

So the rule is conditional on a capture path existing, and it carries the fallback explicitly: say what changed visually and say no capture was available. The **never imply a visual check that was not performed** clause is the load-bearing half — without it the honest fallback and the fabricated one read alike to a model looking for the shortest compliant output. Guarded against the unconditional form specifically, because that form is what regrows: it is what the source guide says, and #71 still carries it.

**Why a decision-only draft is not paste-ready, and why saying so took a whole column (Codex round, Sept 2026):** the item's third field said *"the recommended reply, paste-ready"*, and the attended path said the person may send it. Both are true of an answerable-from-work draft and false of a decision-only one, which deliberately lists options and costs and **makes no pick** — `settle-outstanding-decisions` already stated that *"approve the draft" would record a ruling that chose nothing*. So the branch told a person they could post a non-answer into a review thread, and the thread would then read as handled with the question still undecided, bypassing the one flow built to settle it. The two draft kinds had been split with care where the draft is *produced* and then silently collapsed everywhere the draft is *used* — six sites, which is why this was walked as an axis rather than patched at the line Codex flagged.

**Why two guards on that column were still green over it (same round):** both matched whole-file, and both phrases appear twice — once at the decision point and once in a downstream summary. Mutating the decision point left the summary, and the assertion passed. Scoping them with `clause()` and `near()` is the fix, and it is the same lesson the guard battery's own docstring records from rounds 29-33: presence in a file is not presence where the rule is read.

**Why the item's field list lives in one place (round two, Sept 2026):** the first version of the ruling stated the five fields at the emitting site and then enumerated them again at four consumers. Within one review round three of those copies had dropped a field and a fourth had dropped two — the walkthrough lost *why it was not posted*, which is the field its own next sentence branches on. That is the summary case `CLAUDE.md` says to collapse rather than reconcile, and it is worth naming here because the enumerations all looked like helpful precision. They are now pointers; the list has one home. The evals drifted the same way from the same cause, which is the tell that it was the shape and not four separate slips.

**Why the blanket footer needed a shape detector and not a phrase (round two):** the guard on the discriminator's absolute matched a substring that survived the blanket claim being re-prefixed onto the same sentence — so a stale *"the footer every authored write now carries"* sat at that decision point, green, in the round that introduced the narrowing. The mutation battery found it in one run once a mutation existed. Hence `BLANKET_FOOTER`, which looks for the claim in either word order anywhere in the file: the blanket was the previous rule here, so it is the thing that creeps back, and a positive assertion about the corrected clause cannot detect it.

**Why a question item has required contents (Sept 2026):** the reason the answer is not posted is so that a person can post it. An item that makes them hunt defeats the rule it implements — they open the PR, find the thread, re-read the ask, reconstruct what the pass already knew, and the cost of escalating exceeds the cost of a wrong autonomous answer, which is how a rule like this gets quietly abandoned. Hence five fields and no partial credit: four of five is not four-fifths useful, because the missing one is the one they go looking for.

The `html_url` provenance rule is the one that is not obvious. A review-comment thread and a PR-level comment carry different fragment forms, so a URL assembled from a PR number and a comment id resolves — to the top of the PR, or to a different comment — and **the failure is invisible from the run's side**: the link is well-formed, it returns 200, and only the person clicking it discovers it went nowhere useful. There is no way to detect that from here, which is why the rule is provenance ("the string the API returned") rather than shape ("a valid URL"). It applies to every thread URL the skill emits, not only a question item's, because a no-action entry and a deferred repair name the same threads and are read by the same person.

The notification half is deliberately subordinate. A subscription dies with the session that armed it, so a notification is unobservable-in-principle from a later run: nothing may treat it as delivery, and the item is complete when it is *recorded*. Reporting whether one was sent is useful; depending on it having been seen is the failure mode.

**Why brevity is a rule and not a matter of taste:** length is asymmetric. A run pays nothing to write another paragraph and a reviewer pays for every one, on every PR, and the failure is invisible from inside the run — a PR body that restates the issue and narrates the attempts looks thorough to its author and buries the two lines a reviewer needed. The `Fixed in <sha> — <what changed>.` shape was already the worked example in `resolve-pr-comment`'s reply step; making it the rule is what stops it being read as one illustration among possible others.

**Where the PR-body numbers come from, and why they are numbers:** they are ported from the owner's own PR-description style guide (`baseten/ai-alex`, `writing-style/gh-pr-style.md`, merged as that repository's PR #3), whose standard traces to Stefan's *The crisis of authorship at Close* and Vitor Buzinas's comment on it. The first draft of this section said "a handful of lines", which is the shape of rule that loses every argument with a model that believes its own body is the exceptional case. A word count, a sentence count and a bullet count cannot be reasoned around. The guide itself is a personal file at a local path, so it cannot be cited from skills installed into `~/.claude/skills` or run in a container — hence a self-contained floor here, and the deference rule that lets a repository's or a user's own guide, read through `create-pr`'s existing `CLAUDE.md`/`AGENTS.md` step, override it. Voice rules stay in the personal guide; they are not this section's to state.

**Why *intent, not content* rather than "what changed and why":** that earlier phrasing invited exactly the enumeration the guide forbids, because "what changed" is what a file-by-file inventory answers. The diff already carries the content, and a body competing with it buries the only part a person had to write. The named exclusions — the inventory, the code narration, the investigation log, the journey — are named individually because each is a separate habit and a general injunction to be concise stops none of them.

**The authorship principle these two changes share:** the guide's source argument is *never let AI write a description on your behalf* — generate from the template, then edit it by hand until it sounds like you. That is the same principle as *Handling queries* in `resolve-pr-comment`, arrived at from the other end: a run may do the work of drafting and may not put the result in a person's mouth. The PR body is the case where the person is present to edit, so the rule is a budget and an edit pass; the review thread is the case where they are not, so the rule is that the draft goes to them and nothing is posted. Reading them as two unrelated conventions is how one of them gets relaxed later.

**Why required contents beat brevity rather than the reverse:** every mandated element in this repository exists because dropping it broke something specific — a closing keyword that auto-closes an unfinished issue, a `Depends on:` line the stack topology is read from, a ruling comment a reviewer cannot read the answer out of, a worker report's marker line three dependency readers key on skipping, and the judgment behind it that the report's subtraction exists to carry. A brevity rule that could override any of those would be a licence to reintroduce each of those failures, one short write at a time.
